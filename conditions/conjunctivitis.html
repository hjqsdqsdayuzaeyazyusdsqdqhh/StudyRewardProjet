About Conjunctivitis

Conjunctivitis, or pink eye, is inflammation of the conjunctiva causing redness, itching, and discharge. Clinical trials explore novel antibiotic formulations, antivirals, anti-inflammatory eye drops, and treatments for allergic conjunctivitis.

Common Symptoms

Redness, itching, burning, grittiness, discharge that can be watery or thick and crusty, tearing, swollen eyelids, sensitivity to light

Risk Factors

Exposure to infected individuals, allergies, contact lens wear, environmental irritants, dry eye syndrome, weakened immune system

Treatment Overview

Treatment depends on cause and includes antibiotic drops for bacterial, supportive care for viral, and antihistamine drops for allergic conjunctivitis. Clinical trials study improved topical therapies.

Why Participate?

Conjunctivitis trials provide access to broad-spectrum topical treatments and improved allergy eye drop formulations.

Frequently Asked Questions About Conjunctivitis Clinical Trials

Conjunctivitis clinical trials are research studies that test new treatments, medications, devices, or interventions for managing or treating conjunctivitis. They help determine the safety and effectiveness of potential new approaches.
Eligibility varies by study. Factors include the type and severity of conjunctivitis, age, overall health status, and prior treatment history. Each study has specific inclusion and exclusion criteria.
Compensation depends on the study duration, number of visits, and procedures involved. Participants may receive payments ranging from $50 to $300 per visit, with total compensation varying by study.
All clinical trials follow strict safety protocols and are reviewed by Institutional Review Boards (IRBs) to protect participants. Potential risks are explained during the informed consent process before enrollment.
