About Herpes Simplex

Herpes simplex is a viral infection causing recurrent outbreaks of blisters around the mouth or genitals, with no cure currently available. Clinical trials explore therapeutic vaccines, novel antivirals, gene-editing approaches, and topical treatments for reducing outbreak frequency and transmission.

Common Symptoms

Blisters or sores on the mouth, lips, or genitals, itching or tingling before sores appear, fever, body aches, swollen lymph nodes, pain during urination for genital herpes

Risk Factors

Close contact with infected individuals, multiple sex partners, weakened immune system, stress, illness, sun exposure for oral herpes

Treatment Overview

Antiviral medications such as acyclovir, valacyclovir, and famciclovir reduce outbreak severity and frequency. Clinical trials study therapeutic vaccines and CRISPR-based therapies.

Why Participate?

Herpes trials provide access to potential curative therapies including gene editing and therapeutic vaccines.

Frequently Asked Questions About Herpes Simplex Clinical Trials

Herpes Simplex clinical trials are research studies that test new treatments, medications, devices, or interventions for managing or treating herpes simplex. They help determine the safety and effectiveness of potential new approaches.
Eligibility varies by study. Factors include the type and severity of herpes simplex, age, overall health status, and prior treatment history. Each study has specific inclusion and exclusion criteria.
Compensation depends on the study duration, number of visits, and procedures involved. Participants may receive payments ranging from $50 to $300 per visit, with total compensation varying by study.
All clinical trials follow strict safety protocols and are reviewed by Institutional Review Boards (IRBs) to protect participants. Potential risks are explained during the informed consent process before enrollment.
