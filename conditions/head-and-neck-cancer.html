About Head and Neck Cancer

Head and neck cancers include malignancies of the oral cavity, pharynx, larynx, and sinuses. Clinical trials explore immunotherapies, targeted therapies, organ preservation strategies, and de-escalation approaches to reduce treatment side effects.

Common Symptoms

Persistent sore throat, difficulty swallowing, hoarseness, lump in the neck, ear pain, unexplained weight loss, non-healing mouth sore, white or red patches in the mouth

Risk Factors

Tobacco use, heavy alcohol consumption, HPV infection, poor oral hygiene, male sex, age over 50, exposure to certain chemicals or radiation, Epstein-Barr virus

Treatment Overview

Treatment includes surgery, radiation, chemotherapy, and targeted therapy. Clinical trials study immunotherapy combinations and de-escalation protocols for HPV-positive cancers.

Why Participate?

Head and neck cancer trials provide access to organ-preserving treatments and immunotherapy combinations.

Frequently Asked Questions About Head and Neck Cancer Clinical Trials

Head and Neck Cancer clinical trials are research studies that test new treatments, medications, devices, or interventions for managing or treating head and neck cancer. They help determine the safety and effectiveness of potential new approaches.
Eligibility varies by study. Factors include the type and severity of head and neck cancer, age, overall health status, and prior treatment history. Each study has specific inclusion and exclusion criteria.
Compensation depends on the study duration, number of visits, and procedures involved. Participants may receive payments ranging from $50 to $300 per visit, with total compensation varying by study.
All clinical trials follow strict safety protocols and are reviewed by Institutional Review Boards (IRBs) to protect participants. Potential risks are explained during the informed consent process before enrollment.
