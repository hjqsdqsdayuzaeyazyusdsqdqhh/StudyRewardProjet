About Vaginal Atrophy

Vaginal atrophy, part of the genitourinary syndrome of menopause, involves thinning, drying, and inflammation of the vaginal walls due to estrogen decline. Clinical trials explore low-dose vaginal estrogen, DHEA inserts, ospemifene, laser therapies, and novel moisturizers.

Common Symptoms

Vaginal dryness, burning, discharge, itching, pain during intercourse, urinary urgency, recurrent UTIs, light spotting after sex

Risk Factors

Menopause, breastfeeding, cancer treatments affecting hormones, removal of ovaries, smoking, immune disorders

Treatment Overview

Treatment includes vaginal moisturizers, lubricants, low-dose vaginal estrogen, and ospemifene. Clinical trials study fractional CO2 laser and novel DHEA formulations.

Why Participate?

Vaginal atrophy trials offer access to non-hormonal and minimally invasive treatments for postmenopausal symptoms.

Frequently Asked Questions About Vaginal Atrophy Clinical Trials

Vaginal Atrophy clinical trials are research studies that test new treatments, medications, devices, or interventions for managing or treating vaginal atrophy. They help determine the safety and effectiveness of potential new approaches.
Eligibility varies by study. Factors include the type and severity of vaginal atrophy, age, overall health status, and prior treatment history. Each study has specific inclusion and exclusion criteria.
Compensation depends on the study duration, number of visits, and procedures involved. Participants may receive payments ranging from $50 to $300 per visit, with total compensation varying by study.
All clinical trials follow strict safety protocols and are reviewed by Institutional Review Boards (IRBs) to protect participants. Potential risks are explained during the informed consent process before enrollment.
