About Gastroparesis

Gastroparesis is a condition where the stomach cannot empty food properly due to nerve damage or muscular dysfunction, causing chronic digestive symptoms. Clinical trials explore prokinetic agents, gastric electrical stimulation, pyloromyotomy techniques, and dietary interventions.

Common Symptoms

Nausea, vomiting undigested food, feeling full quickly while eating, bloating, abdominal pain, poor appetite, weight loss, malnutrition, unstable blood sugar in diabetics

Risk Factors

Diabetes, abdominal surgery, viral infections, eating disorders, certain medications including GLP-1 agonists, neurological conditions such as Parkinson disease, hypothyroidism

Treatment Overview

Treatment includes dietary modifications, antiemetics, prokinetic medications, and gastric electrical stimulation. Clinical trials study novel prokinetic agents and endoscopic pyloric interventions.

Why Participate?

Gastroparesis trials offer access to advanced treatments for this difficult-to-manage condition, including gastric stimulation innovations.

Frequently Asked Questions About Gastroparesis Clinical Trials

Gastroparesis clinical trials are research studies that test new treatments, medications, devices, or interventions for managing or treating gastroparesis. They help determine the safety and effectiveness of potential new approaches.
Eligibility varies by study. Factors include the type and severity of gastroparesis, age, overall health status, and prior treatment history. Each study has specific inclusion and exclusion criteria.
Compensation depends on the study duration, number of visits, and procedures involved. Participants may receive payments ranging from $50 to $300 per visit, with total compensation varying by study.
All clinical trials follow strict safety protocols and are reviewed by Institutional Review Boards (IRBs) to protect participants. Potential risks are explained during the informed consent process before enrollment.
