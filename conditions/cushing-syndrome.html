About Cushing Syndrome

Cushing syndrome results from prolonged exposure to high cortisol levels, causing a distinctive set of symptoms and health complications. Clinical trials explore novel steroidogenesis inhibitors, glucocorticoid receptor antagonists, and pituitary-directed therapies.

Common Symptoms

Weight gain especially around the face and abdomen, fatty hump between shoulders, pink or purple stretch marks, thin easily bruised skin, slow healing, acne, fatigue, muscle weakness, high blood pressure, mood changes

Risk Factors

Long-term corticosteroid use, pituitary tumors, adrenal tumors, ectopic ACTH-producing tumors, female sex, age 20-50

Treatment Overview

Treatment depends on the cause and may include surgery, radiation, and medications. Clinical trials study oral steroidogenesis inhibitors and glucocorticoid receptor antagonists.

Why Participate?

Cushing syndrome trials provide access to targeted medical therapies that may avoid surgery or manage persistent disease.

Frequently Asked Questions About Cushing Syndrome Clinical Trials

Cushing Syndrome clinical trials are research studies that test new treatments, medications, devices, or interventions for managing or treating cushing syndrome. They help determine the safety and effectiveness of potential new approaches.
Eligibility varies by study. Factors include the type and severity of cushing syndrome, age, overall health status, and prior treatment history. Each study has specific inclusion and exclusion criteria.
Compensation depends on the study duration, number of visits, and procedures involved. Participants may receive payments ranging from $50 to $300 per visit, with total compensation varying by study.
All clinical trials follow strict safety protocols and are reviewed by Institutional Review Boards (IRBs) to protect participants. Potential risks are explained during the informed consent process before enrollment.
