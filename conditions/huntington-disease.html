About Huntington Disease

Huntington disease is a genetic neurodegenerative disorder causing progressive motor, cognitive, and psychiatric symptoms. Clinical trials explore gene silencing therapies, antisense oligonucleotides, and disease-modifying approaches targeting the huntingtin protein.

Common Symptoms

Involuntary jerking movements, muscle rigidity, difficulty walking, impaired coordination, difficulty speaking or swallowing, cognitive decline, depression, irritability, obsessive-compulsive behaviors

Risk Factors

Family history, inheritance of the mutated HTT gene, age typically 30-50 at onset, European ancestry

Treatment Overview

No cure exists. Treatment focuses on symptom management. Clinical trials study huntingtin-lowering therapies including ASOs and gene therapy approaches.

Why Participate?

Huntington disease trials provide access to potentially disease-modifying gene-targeted therapies.

Frequently Asked Questions About Huntington Disease Clinical Trials

Huntington Disease clinical trials are research studies that test new treatments, medications, devices, or interventions for managing or treating huntington disease. They help determine the safety and effectiveness of potential new approaches.
Eligibility varies by study. Factors include the type and severity of huntington disease, age, overall health status, and prior treatment history. Each study has specific inclusion and exclusion criteria.
Compensation depends on the study duration, number of visits, and procedures involved. Participants may receive payments ranging from $50 to $300 per visit, with total compensation varying by study.
All clinical trials follow strict safety protocols and are reviewed by Institutional Review Boards (IRBs) to protect participants. Potential risks are explained during the informed consent process before enrollment.
