About Sciatica

Sciatica refers to pain radiating along the sciatic nerve path from the lower back through the hips and down each leg, usually caused by a herniated disc or bone spur. Clinical trials explore epidural steroid injections, surgical techniques, physical therapy approaches, and regenerative treatments.

Common Symptoms

Pain radiating from lower back to buttock and down the leg, numbness or tingling in the leg, muscle weakness, burning sensation, difficulty moving the leg or foot, sharp pain when sitting or coughing

Risk Factors

Age, obesity, prolonged sitting, occupation requiring heavy lifting or twisting, diabetes, herniated disc, spinal stenosis

Treatment Overview

Treatment includes physical therapy, NSAIDs, epidural steroid injections, and surgery for severe cases. Clinical trials study microdiscectomy techniques and regenerative injection therapies.

Why Participate?

Sciatica trials offer access to minimally invasive surgical techniques and biologic therapies for nerve pain relief.

Frequently Asked Questions About Sciatica Clinical Trials

Sciatica clinical trials are research studies that test new treatments, medications, devices, or interventions for managing or treating sciatica. They help determine the safety and effectiveness of potential new approaches.
Eligibility varies by study. Factors include the type and severity of sciatica, age, overall health status, and prior treatment history. Each study has specific inclusion and exclusion criteria.
Compensation depends on the study duration, number of visits, and procedures involved. Participants may receive payments ranging from $50 to $300 per visit, with total compensation varying by study.
All clinical trials follow strict safety protocols and are reviewed by Institutional Review Boards (IRBs) to protect participants. Potential risks are explained during the informed consent process before enrollment.
