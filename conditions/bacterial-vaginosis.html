About Bacterial Vaginosis

Bacterial vaginosis is a common vaginal infection caused by an imbalance of naturally occurring bacteria, causing discharge and odor. Clinical trials explore novel antibiotic formulations, probiotic therapies, biofilm-disrupting agents, and pH-modulating treatments.

Common Symptoms

Thin gray or white vaginal discharge, strong fishy odor especially after sex, vaginal itching, burning during urination

Risk Factors

Multiple sex partners, douching, smoking, intrauterine device use, lack of lactobacilli, new or multiple sex partners, antibiotic use

Treatment Overview

Treatment includes metronidazole and clindamycin. Clinical trials study biofilm-disrupting agents and probiotic combinations to prevent recurrence.

Why Participate?

Bacterial vaginosis trials provide access to treatments that may reduce the high recurrence rate of this condition.

Frequently Asked Questions About Bacterial Vaginosis Clinical Trials

Bacterial Vaginosis clinical trials are research studies that test new treatments, medications, devices, or interventions for managing or treating bacterial vaginosis. They help determine the safety and effectiveness of potential new approaches.
Eligibility varies by study. Factors include the type and severity of bacterial vaginosis, age, overall health status, and prior treatment history. Each study has specific inclusion and exclusion criteria.
Compensation depends on the study duration, number of visits, and procedures involved. Participants may receive payments ranging from $50 to $300 per visit, with total compensation varying by study.
All clinical trials follow strict safety protocols and are reviewed by Institutional Review Boards (IRBs) to protect participants. Potential risks are explained during the informed consent process before enrollment.
