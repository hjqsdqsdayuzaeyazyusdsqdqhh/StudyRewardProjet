About Heart Valve Disease

Heart valve disease occurs when one or more heart valves do not function properly, disrupting blood flow through the heart. Clinical trials explore transcatheter valve replacements, valve repair innovations, and novel anticoagulation strategies.

Common Symptoms

Shortness of breath, fatigue, heart murmur, chest pain, irregular heartbeat, swelling of feet or ankles, fainting, rapid weight gain from fluid retention

Risk Factors

Advancing age, congenital heart disease, history of rheumatic fever, endocarditis, high blood pressure, cholesterol, diabetes, radiation therapy to chest

Treatment Overview

Treatment includes medications, balloon valvuloplasty, and valve repair or replacement. Clinical trials study transcatheter mitral and tricuspid valve interventions.

Why Participate?

Heart valve disease trials offer access to minimally invasive transcatheter procedures and next-generation valve technologies.

Frequently Asked Questions About Heart Valve Disease Clinical Trials

Heart Valve Disease clinical trials are research studies that test new treatments, medications, devices, or interventions for managing or treating heart valve disease. They help determine the safety and effectiveness of potential new approaches.
Eligibility varies by study. Factors include the type and severity of heart valve disease, age, overall health status, and prior treatment history. Each study has specific inclusion and exclusion criteria.
Compensation depends on the study duration, number of visits, and procedures involved. Participants may receive payments ranging from $50 to $300 per visit, with total compensation varying by study.
All clinical trials follow strict safety protocols and are reviewed by Institutional Review Boards (IRBs) to protect participants. Potential risks are explained during the informed consent process before enrollment.
