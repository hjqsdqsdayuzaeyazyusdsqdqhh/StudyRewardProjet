About Prostatitis

Prostatitis is inflammation of the prostate gland, causing pain and urinary symptoms. It can be acute or chronic and bacterial or nonbacterial. Clinical trials explore targeted antibiotics, anti-inflammatory therapies, alpha-blockers, and pelvic floor physical therapy approaches.

Common Symptoms

Painful or frequent urination, difficulty urinating, pain in the groin, lower back, or genitals, fever and chills for acute cases, painful ejaculation, urgent need to urinate

Risk Factors

Young to middle-aged men, previous prostatitis, urinary tract infections, pelvic trauma, catheter use, dehydration, stress

Treatment Overview

Treatment includes antibiotics, alpha-blockers, anti-inflammatory medications, and pain management. Clinical trials study novel anti-inflammatory agents and neuromodulation for chronic pelvic pain.

Why Participate?

Prostatitis trials offer access to targeted treatments for chronic pelvic pain and improved antibiotic regimens.

Frequently Asked Questions About Prostatitis Clinical Trials

Prostatitis clinical trials are research studies that test new treatments, medications, devices, or interventions for managing or treating prostatitis. They help determine the safety and effectiveness of potential new approaches.
Eligibility varies by study. Factors include the type and severity of prostatitis, age, overall health status, and prior treatment history. Each study has specific inclusion and exclusion criteria.
Compensation depends on the study duration, number of visits, and procedures involved. Participants may receive payments ranging from $50 to $300 per visit, with total compensation varying by study.
All clinical trials follow strict safety protocols and are reviewed by Institutional Review Boards (IRBs) to protect participants. Potential risks are explained during the informed consent process before enrollment.
