About Pelvic Inflammatory Disease

Pelvic inflammatory disease is an infection of the female reproductive organs, often caused by sexually transmitted infections. It can lead to serious complications including infertility. Clinical trials explore improved antibiotic regimens, outpatient treatment protocols, and prevention strategies.

Common Symptoms

Lower abdominal or pelvic pain, abnormal vaginal discharge with odor, fever, pain during intercourse or urination, irregular menstrual bleeding, feeling tired or unwell

Risk Factors

Multiple sex partners, age under 25, history of STIs, intrauterine device insertion, douching, previous PID episode

Treatment Overview

Treatment includes broad-spectrum antibiotics. Clinical trials study novel antibiotic combinations and adjunctive anti-inflammatory therapies to reduce long-term complications.

Why Participate?

PID trials provide access to optimized treatment protocols that may reduce the risk of infertility and chronic pelvic pain.

Frequently Asked Questions About Pelvic Inflammatory Disease Clinical Trials

Pelvic Inflammatory Disease clinical trials are research studies that test new treatments, medications, devices, or interventions for managing or treating pelvic inflammatory disease. They help determine the safety and effectiveness of potential new approaches.
Eligibility varies by study. Factors include the type and severity of pelvic inflammatory disease, age, overall health status, and prior treatment history. Each study has specific inclusion and exclusion criteria.
Compensation depends on the study duration, number of visits, and procedures involved. Participants may receive payments ranging from $50 to $300 per visit, with total compensation varying by study.
All clinical trials follow strict safety protocols and are reviewed by Institutional Review Boards (IRBs) to protect participants. Potential risks are explained during the informed consent process before enrollment.
