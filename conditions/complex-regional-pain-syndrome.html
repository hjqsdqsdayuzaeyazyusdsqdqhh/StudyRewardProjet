About Complex Regional Pain Syndrome

Complex regional pain syndrome is a chronic pain condition typically affecting a limb after an injury, characterized by prolonged pain and sensitivity. Clinical trials explore nerve blocks, spinal cord stimulation, ketamine infusions, mirror therapy, and novel anti-inflammatory agents.

Common Symptoms

Continuous burning or throbbing pain, swelling, changes in skin temperature and color, increased sensitivity to touch, joint stiffness, muscle spasms and weakness, reduced range of motion

Risk Factors

Fractures, surgeries, sprains or strains, immobilization, certain genetic factors, age 40-60, female sex

Treatment Overview

Treatment includes physical therapy, medications such as gabapentin and corticosteroids, and nerve blocks. Clinical trials study spinal cord stimulation and novel immunomodulatory approaches.

Why Participate?

CRPS trials provide access to advanced neuromodulation techniques and interventional pain management options.

Frequently Asked Questions About Complex Regional Pain Syndrome Clinical Trials

Complex Regional Pain Syndrome clinical trials are research studies that test new treatments, medications, devices, or interventions for managing or treating complex regional pain syndrome. They help determine the safety and effectiveness of potential new approaches.
Eligibility varies by study. Factors include the type and severity of complex regional pain syndrome, age, overall health status, and prior treatment history. Each study has specific inclusion and exclusion criteria.
Compensation depends on the study duration, number of visits, and procedures involved. Participants may receive payments ranging from $50 to $300 per visit, with total compensation varying by study.
All clinical trials follow strict safety protocols and are reviewed by Institutional Review Boards (IRBs) to protect participants. Potential risks are explained during the informed consent process before enrollment.
