Clinical Trial Withdrawal: Legal and Practical Considerations

Your rights and responsibilities when withdrawing from a clinical trial, including legal implications and practical steps.

By Attorney Rachel Green May 16, 2026

Your rights and responsibilities when withdrawing from a clinical trial, including legal implications and practical steps.

Your Right to Withdraw at Any Time

Every clinical trial participant has the unconditional right to withdraw from the study at any time, for any reason, without penalty. This right is protected by ethical guidelines including the Declaration of Helsinki and federal research regulations. Withdrawal will not affect your access to regular medical care or any benefits you are entitled to outside the study. You do not need to provide a reason for leaving, though sharing your concerns may help the research team improve.

Steps for Withdrawing Properly

Notify your study coordinator or principal investigator in writing if you decide to withdraw. A brief email or letter stating your decision to withdraw is sufficient. Return any study equipment or unused medication as instructed. Complete an end-of-study visit if one is offered, as this allows the team to assess your health status before you leave. Ask about any follow-up care or monitoring that may be recommended after withdrawal.

What Happens to Your Data After Withdrawal

When you withdraw, you can request that no new data be collected from you. However, data already collected up to the point of withdrawal may still be used in the study analysis. You can also request that your biological samples be destroyed if you wish. Your withdrawal decision and any data usage limitations should be documented. The informed consent form should specify how data will be handled if you withdraw.

Addressing Concerns That Lead to Withdrawal

If you are considering withdrawal due to concerns about side effects, schedule demands, or other issues, discuss these with your coordinator first. Many concerns can be addressed through schedule adjustments, symptom management, or additional support resources. If you do decide to withdraw, share your reasons constructively to help the research team improve future participant experiences.

Key Takeaways

  • You have the unconditional right to withdraw from a clinical trial at any time without penalty.
  • Notify your coordinator in writing and return any study materials or unused medication.
  • Data already collected may be used, but you can request no further data collection and sample destruction.
  • Discuss concerns with your coordinator before withdrawing, as many issues can be resolved.
