Clinical Trial Adverse Events and Serious Adverse Events

Understanding adverse events in clinical trials, how they are classified, reported, and what they mean for participant safety.

By Dr. Sarah Mitchell April 22, 2026

Understanding adverse events in clinical trials, how they are classified, reported, and what they mean for participant safety.

What Are Adverse Events in Clinical Trials?

An adverse event is any unfavorable medical occurrence that happens during a clinical trial, whether or not it is related to the study treatment. Adverse events can include symptoms like headache or nausea, abnormal laboratory values, or new diagnoses. Not all adverse events are caused by the study treatment. Some may result from the participant's underlying condition or be coincidental occurrences. Researchers carefully document all adverse events regardless of suspected cause.

Classifying Adverse Events by Severity

Adverse events are graded on a scale from mild to severe. Mild events cause minimal discomfort and do not interfere with daily activities. Moderate events cause enough discomfort to interfere with normal activities. Severe events make it impossible to perform regular activities. Serious adverse events are a separate category that includes events resulting in death, life-threatening situations, hospitalization, disability, or birth defects. All serious adverse events must be reported to regulators within specific timeframes.

How Adverse Events Are Reported and Tracked

Participants report adverse events to their study coordinator, who documents them in the study records. The research team assesses each event's severity, duration, and relationship to the study treatment. Adverse events are coded using standardized medical terminology for consistent analysis. Independent Data Safety Monitoring Boards review adverse event data periodically to identify safety concerns. Regulatory agencies also review adverse event reports to monitor study safety.

What Adverse Event Data Means for Participants

When considering a clinical trial, review the types and rates of adverse events reported in previous studies of the same treatment. Common adverse events occur in more than 10 percent of participants, while rare events occur in less than 1 percent. Discuss potential adverse events with your coordinator and understand which symptoms warrant immediate medical attention. Remember that adverse event reporting ensures transparency and ongoing safety monitoring throughout the study.

Key Takeaways

  • Adverse events are any unfavorable medical occurrences during a trial, whether or not related to the treatment.
  • Events are graded as mild, moderate, or severe, with serious adverse events requiring immediate regulatory reporting.
  • Participants report events to coordinators; independent boards review data to identify safety concerns.
  • Review adverse event profiles when considering a trial and discuss potential risks with your coordinator.
