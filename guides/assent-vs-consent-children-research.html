Assent vs. Consent: Children's Role in Research Decisions

Understanding the difference between parental consent and child assent in pediatric clinical trials and children's rights in research.

By Attorney Rachel Green April 10, 2026

Understanding the difference between parental consent and child assent in pediatric clinical trials and children's rights in research.

What Is Parental Permission in Research?

Parental permission is the legal authorization for a child to participate in research. One or both parents must sign a permission document after receiving complete information about the study. The permission document explains the study's purpose, procedures, risks, and benefits in clear language. Parents have the right to ask questions and take time before deciding. Parental permission is legally required for all research involving minors.

Understanding Child Assent

Assent is a child's affirmative agreement to participate in research. Children who are capable of understanding the study in basic terms should have the opportunity to provide or withhold their assent. The assent process uses age-appropriate language and materials. For younger children, assent may be expressed verbally or through behavior. For older children and adolescents, written assent may be obtained. A child's objection to participation must be respected, even if parents have given permission.

Age-Appropriate Approaches to Assent

Researchers tailor the assent process to each child's developmental level. For children ages 7 to 12, researchers use simple language, pictures, and stories to explain what will happen. For adolescents ages 13 to 17, the process resembles adult informed consent with age-appropriate explanations. Children are told that participation is voluntary and they can stop at any time. The assent process respects the child's evolving capacity to make independent decisions.

When Assent May Be Waived

In some circumstances, assent may not be required. Children who are too young or have cognitive impairments that prevent meaningful understanding may be exempted from the assent process. However, their dissent, or objection, must still be respected. Studies that offer direct benefit that is not otherwise available may proceed with parental permission alone if the child cannot meaningfully participate in the assent process. These determinations are made by the IRB during protocol review.

Key Takeaways

  • Parental permission is the legal authorization required for all research involving minors.
  • Child assent is the affirmative agreement to participate, obtained using age-appropriate language and materials.
  • A child's objection to participation must be respected even if parents have given permission.
  • Assent processes are tailored to developmental level, with different approaches for children and adolescents.
