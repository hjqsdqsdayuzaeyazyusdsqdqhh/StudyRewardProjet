HIPAA and Clinical Trials: Your Privacy Rights

How HIPAA protects your health information during clinical trial participation and what privacy rights you have as a research participant.

By Attorney Rachel Green May 19, 2026

How HIPAA protects your health information during clinical trial participation and what privacy rights you have as a research participant.

Understanding HIPAA in the Research Context

HIPAA, the Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act, establishes national standards for protecting individuals' medical records and personal health information. In clinical trials, HIPAA requires researchers to obtain your written authorization before using or sharing your health information for research purposes. This authorization is typically combined with the informed consent document. HIPAA applies to all research conducted by covered entities such as hospitals, clinics, and health plans.

What Information Is Protected

HIPAA protects individually identifiable health information including your name, address, dates of birth and treatment, medical history, test results, and genetic information. Any information that could reasonably identify you as an individual is considered protected health information or PHI. Researchers must de-identify data by removing all 18 categories of identifiers before sharing information externally. De-identified data is no longer subject to HIPAA restrictions.

Your Rights Under HIPAA

As a research participant, you have the right to request access to your health information held by the research site. You can request amendments to incorrect information and receive an accounting of who has accessed your data. You have the right to revoke your HIPAA authorization at any time, though information already shared based on your authorization may continue to be used. The research site must provide you with a Notice of Privacy Practices explaining these rights.

How Researchers Protect Your Data

Research sites implement multiple safeguards to protect participant data. Physical safeguards include locked file cabinets and secure facilities. Technical safeguards include encryption, password protection, and secure data transmission systems. Administrative safeguards include staff training on privacy policies and procedures. Researchers use the minimum necessary standard, only accessing the information needed for their specific research role.

Key Takeaways

  • HIPAA requires written authorization before researchers can use or share your protected health information.
  • All individually identifiable health information is protected, including medical history, test results, and genetic data.
  • You have rights to access, amend, and request accounting of disclosures of your health information.
  • Researchers use physical, technical, and administrative safeguards to protect participant data.
