School and Clinical Trial Participation: A Guide for Families

How to manage school commitments and clinical trial participation for your child, including communication with teachers and administrators.

By Lisa Thompson April 7, 2026

How to manage school commitments and clinical trial participation for your child, including communication with teachers and administrators.

Communicating with Your Child's School

Inform your child's teacher and school administration about the clinical trial participation. Provide a letter from the research team explaining the study in general terms, visit schedules, and any activity restrictions. Request that missed school time be excused as medical appointments. Discuss how the school can support your child during participation, including medication management if applicable. Maintaining open communication with the school helps ensure your child's educational needs are met.

Managing Missed School Time

Clinical trial visits may require your child to miss some school hours. Plan visits during less critical academic times when possible, such as after standardized testing periods. Request homework in advance so your child can keep up with assignments. Work with the research team to schedule visits before or after school hours if available. Keep track of missed instructional time and communicate with teachers about how your child can make up missed work.

Supporting Your Child Through the Process

Prepare your child for study visits by explaining what will happen in simple, reassuring terms. Allow your child to bring a comfort item or favorite activity to visits. Listen to your child's concerns about missing school activities or feeling different from peers. Praise your child for their bravery and contribution to research. Maintain normal routines as much as possible to provide stability.

When to Reevaluate Participation

Monitor your child's academic performance and emotional well-being throughout the trial. If participation significantly affects school performance or causes persistent distress, discuss concerns with the research team. Some studies can accommodate schedule modifications or provide additional support. If the burden becomes too great, remember that you can withdraw your child from the study at any time without affecting their regular medical care.

Key Takeaways

  • Communicate with the school early, providing a study letter and discussing how the school can support your child.
  • Plan visits to minimize missed school time and coordinate with teachers for make-up work.
  • Prepare and support your child emotionally, maintaining normal routines and listening to their concerns.
  • Monitor academic and emotional well-being throughout participation, and withdraw if the burden becomes excessive.

Frequently Asked Questions

Most schools excuse clinical trial visits as medical appointments when documentation is provided. Check your school district's attendance policy.
Disclosure is a personal decision. Some families choose to share information, while others prefer privacy. Do what feels comfortable for your family.
School medication policies vary. Work with the research team and school nurse to determine the safest approach for any study medications.
